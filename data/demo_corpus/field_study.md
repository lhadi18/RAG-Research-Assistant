# Synthetic Urban Garden Field Study (Demonstration Corpus)

This self-authored example is synthetic and illustrates research-note handling. It is not a real study or scientific publication.

## Protocol
In a fictional six-week observation, two rooftop planters were watered using different schedules. Planter A received a fixed daily volume; Planter B was watered when the top layer felt dry. The exercise records protocol design only and does not establish a general horticultural result. The planters differed in sunlight exposure, so sunlight is a potential confounder.

## Observations
The fictional log reports that Planter A required more water over the observation period. The notes do not provide calibrated soil moisture readings, replicated plots, or a statistical analysis. Leaf counts were recorded inconsistently. These limitations prevent a reliable causal conclusion about watering schedule and plant growth.

## Interpretation limits
The example supports the narrow statement that the fictional observation log recorded greater water use for Planter A. It does not show that a fixed schedule is worse, that condition-based watering improves yield, or that the result generalizes to other climates or plants. A stronger experiment would randomize replicated planters, control light and soil, define outcome measures in advance, and publish the raw data.
